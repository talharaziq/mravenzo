Knowing **how to prune a Monstera plant** helps you remove damaged leaves, control a vine that has outgrown its space and prepare healthy stem sections for propagation. The key is to understand the difference between a leaf stalk and the main stem. A leaf on its own will not make a new plant, and a cut in the wrong place can remove the node needed for future growth.

For the wider picture, start with our [Monstera plant guide](/articles/monstera-plant-guide/), which brings together identification, everyday care and common problems.

For most indoor Monsteras, light pruning is enough. Remove dead or badly damaged leaves as needed, and make larger size-control cuts when the plant is actively growing. Use clean, sharp tools and avoid removing too much healthy foliage at once.

This guide focuses on *Monstera deliciosa*, although the same basic cutting principles apply to several climbing aroids. For general growing advice, see our [Monstera plant care guide](/articles/monstera-plant-care/).

## When should you prune a Monstera?

Spring and early summer are convenient times for structural pruning because the plant is usually entering a period of active growth. A healthy plant can respond more readily than one that is already stressed by winter conditions, root rot or pests. However, you do not need to wait for spring to remove a fully dead or diseased leaf. Remove damaged tissue when necessary, using clean tools.

Prune when:

- A leaf is fully yellow, brown or badly damaged.
- A stem has grown beyond the space available.
- The plant is leaning or spreading in a way that cannot be managed with a support.
- You want a healthy cutting with a node for propagation.
- Dead or damaged growth needs to be removed during pest or disease management.

Avoid heavy pruning immediately after repotting, during severe stress or when the plant has very few healthy leaves. A Monstera needs its foliage to make energy. Removing many green leaves at once can slow recovery.

## Understand the parts before you cut

A Monstera has a main stem or vine, leaf stalks called petioles, leaf blades, nodes and aerial roots. A **node** is the point on the stem where a leaf and other growth structures emerge. It may look like a small joint or slightly raised area. New shoots and roots can develop from nodes.

A leaf blade is attached to a petiole, and the petiole attaches to the main stem. If you cut off only a leaf and its petiole, you have removed foliage, but you have not made a stem cutting that can grow into another plant. For propagation, a cutting needs a section of stem with at least one node and a viable growth point. The [University of Minnesota Extension propagation guide](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/propagating-monstera-deliciosa) explains why a leaf without a node will not produce new growth.

## Tools and preparation

Use sharp bypass pruners or clean gardening scissors for thin stems. A thicker stem may need a stronger, sharp pruning tool. Wipe soil and plant residue from the blades, then disinfect them before cutting and again when moving between plants. A clean blade makes a neater cut and reduces the chance of transferring pests or pathogens.

Wear gloves if your skin is sensitive, and keep cuttings away from children and pets. *Monstera deliciosa* contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals and is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. Place the removed leaves in a closed waste container rather than leaving them on the floor.

Before making a cut, decide whether you are removing one leaf, shortening a vine or taking a propagation cutting. Mark the point mentally and check that you are not cutting through a node you intend to keep.

## How to remove a yellow or damaged leaf

If a leaf is fully yellow or mostly dead, follow its petiole back to where it joins the main stem. Cut the petiole close to its base, taking care not to nick the main stem. Do not pull the leaf off by hand because pulling can tear the stem tissue.

A partly yellow leaf may still contribute energy. If only the tip is damaged, you can leave it in place or trim the dry edge for appearance, following the natural leaf outline. Removing a leaf will not solve the cause of the yellowing. Check watering, drainage, light and roots first. Our guide to [why Monstera leaves turn yellow](/articles/monstera-yellow-leaves/) explains the main causes.

## How to shorten an overgrown Monstera stem

Choose a healthy section of the vine and locate the node nearest the length you want to keep. If you want the remaining plant to continue growing from a lower node, make a clean cut just above that node, leaving the node attached to the rooted part of the plant. The exact cut position can vary with the shape of the stem, so avoid cutting directly through the node.

If you are removing an entire stem, cut it near the point where it joins the main plant without damaging the rest of the plant. Do not confuse a leaf petiole with a separate stem. A large *M. deliciosa* can have several leaves emerging along one vine; removing one petiole does not shorten the vine itself.

Take care not to remove every growing tip. If you want to reduce the overall size, you may be able to guide a long stem onto a support instead of cutting it. See [how to support a Monstera plant](/articles/support-monstera-plant/) for options such as a moss pole, plank or trellis.

## How to prune a Monstera for propagation

If you want a new plant, choose a healthy stem section that includes at least one node and preferably one healthy leaf. Cut a little below the node so that the node remains on the cutting. Keep the node visible and do not remove it when trimming away a damaged leaf.

A single leaf with only a petiole will not grow into a new Monstera because it lacks the stem node and axillary bud needed for new growth. This is a common reason cuttings fail. For complete instructions on rooting in water, potting mix or by air layering, follow [how to propagate a Monstera plant](/articles/propagate-monstera-plant/).

## Should you cut off aerial roots?

Aerial roots are a normal part of Monstera growth. They help the plant attach to a climbing surface and, when they reach suitable material, can contribute to support and water uptake. You do not need to cut them simply because they look unusual.

If an aerial root is flexible, guide it toward a moss pole or into the potting mix. Avoid bending a thick, brittle root sharply. You can trim a root that is dead, damaged or creating a practical problem, but do not remove healthy roots routinely. If you are planning to repot the plant, consider arranging the roots during that process rather than repeatedly cutting them back.

## What to do after pruning

Keep the plant in its usual bright, indirect light and continue checking soil moisture before watering. Do not increase fertiliser immediately to “replace” the leaves. A balanced feed during active growth is enough when the plant's roots are healthy. Avoid heavy watering after pruning unless the soil actually needs it.

Watch the remaining stems and nodes for new growth over the following weeks. Growth speed depends on the season, temperature, light and plant health. A cut stem may not produce a new shoot immediately. If the plant is stable and the remaining foliage looks healthy, give it time rather than making more cuts.

If the pruning has left the plant unstable, secure the main stem with a support. If several stems have roots and you want separate plants, division may be possible during repotting. Read [how to repot and split a Monstera](/articles/repot-monstera-plant/).

## Frequently asked questions about pruning a Monstera

### Where do I cut a Monstera plant?

For size control, identify the main stem and a node you want to keep, then make a clean cut just above that node. To remove a leaf, cut its petiole near the main stem without cutting into the stem itself.

### Can I cut the top off my Monstera?

You can shorten the main vine by cutting above a node on the rooted plant. The removed top can be used as a cutting if it includes a node and viable growth point. Avoid cutting so low that the remaining plant has no healthy growth points.

### Will a Monstera grow back after pruning?

A healthy plant may produce new growth from a node below the cut. The timing and number of new shoots vary with plant health, season and growing conditions.

### Can I cut off aerial roots on a Monstera?

You can remove a damaged or disruptive aerial root, but healthy roots are useful for climbing and support. Guide them toward a moss pole or potting mix where practical instead of cutting them routinely.

### Can I prune a Monstera in winter?

Remove dead or badly damaged leaves whenever needed. For major size reduction, spring or early summer is usually easier because the plant is more likely to be actively growing. Avoid heavy pruning while the plant is already stressed.

### Can I use a leaf cutting to grow a new Monstera?

A leaf and petiole without a node will not produce a new growing stem. Use a stem cutting that includes a node and an axillary bud. See our [propagation guide](/articles/propagate-monstera-plant/).

### Should I seal a Monstera pruning cut?

For ordinary houseplant pruning, a clean cut is generally left to dry naturally. Do not apply household glue, wax or unlabelled wound products. Keep the plant in suitable conditions and avoid getting the cut wet repeatedly.

### How much of a Monstera can I prune at once?

There is no universal safe percentage for every plant. Remove dead leaves as needed, but avoid taking many healthy leaves from a small or stressed plant in one session. For a large size reduction, consider staged pruning.
