Good **Monstera plant care** starts with three things: bright indirect light, an airy potting mix and careful watering. A Monstera does not need water on the same day every week. It needs water when the top of its mix has started to dry, and it needs a container that allows excess water to escape. Once those basics are right, feeding, cleaning and support are straightforward.

This guide focuses mainly on *Monstera deliciosa*, the large-leaved Swiss cheese plant commonly grown indoors. Some advice also applies to other Monstera species, but smaller vines and variegated forms can differ in their growth rate and light needs. For identification, start with our [Monstera plant types guide](/articles/monstera-plant-types/). You can also use the broader [Monstera plant guide](/articles/monstera-plant-guide/) as a reference for the whole group.

## 1. Give your Monstera the right light

Place the plant in bright, indirect light. A bright room near an east-facing window is often suitable. Near a strong south- or west-facing window, move the pot back from the glass or use a sheer curtain to soften direct sun. The best position depends on the strength of the light, window exposure and season.

A Monstera kept in a dark corner may survive, but growth is usually slower and stems can become stretched as they reach toward the light. New leaves may be smaller and may develop fewer holes or splits. If the plant leans strongly in one direction, rotate it a little every few weeks rather than turning it sharply every day.

Direct sun is not automatically harmful in every setting. A plant acclimatised to gentle morning sun may tolerate it. However, sudden exposure to hot midday or afternoon sun can cause pale, dry scorch patches. Make changes gradually and watch the leaves over the following week.

Variegated forms such as Thai Constellation or Albo usually need a brighter position than all-green plants because the pale leaf areas have less chlorophyll. They still need protection from intense direct sun. More light can support healthy growth; it cannot turn a white leaf section green.

## 2. Water when the upper soil has dried

The safest routine is to check the mix before watering. Press a finger a few centimetres into the soil. If the surface and upper layer feel dry, and the pot feels lighter, it may be time to water. If the mix is still cool and damp, wait and check again later. A wooden skewer inserted into the root zone can help you judge moisture in a large pot.

When watering, pour slowly and evenly until water runs from the drainage holes. Let the pot drain fully, then empty the saucer or decorative outer pot. Do not leave the plant standing in water. A little water at the base of a cachepot can keep the lower soil wet long after the top appears dry.

The interval changes with the season and the room. A plant in bright light and warm air may dry quickly in spring and summer. A plant in a cool room during winter can remain wet for much longer. Pot size, root density and soil texture also matter. This is why fixed advice such as “water every seven days” can lead to overwatering.

Too much water is a common cause of poor root health. The roots need both moisture and air. If the potting mix stays saturated, roots can become damaged and leaves may turn yellow or wilt even though the soil is wet. For symptoms and a diagnosis checklist, see [why Monstera leaves turn yellow](/articles/monstera-yellow-leaves/).

## 3. Choose an airy potting mix

Monstera roots need a mix that retains some moisture but does not stay dense and waterlogged. A useful starting blend is two parts quality indoor potting mix, one part orchid bark and one part perlite or pumice. Treat that as an example rather than a universal formula; the right balance depends on how quickly your room dries the pot and how often you tend to water.

A mix that is too dense can hold water around the roots. A mix that is extremely coarse may dry before the plant can use the moisture. Check the result after watering: water should move through the mix, but the root ball should not dry out immediately.

Use a pot with open drainage holes. Terracotta can dry faster than plastic or glazed ceramic, while a plastic pot may retain moisture for longer. Neither is automatically better. Choose based on your watering habits and the conditions in your room.

## 4. Keep the temperature steady

Monstera is a tropical plant and prefers a warm, stable room. Normal indoor temperatures are generally suitable. Avoid cold draughts, sudden temperature changes and direct contact with heaters or air-conditioning vents. Cold, wet soil is especially unhelpful because it slows water use while roots remain exposed to excess moisture.

The Royal Horticultural Society describes Monsteras as warm-growing houseplants that prefer indirect light and humid conditions. Its [Swiss cheese plant guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/swiss-cheese-plants) gives a useful overview of preferred conditions.

## 5. Manage humidity without keeping the soil wet

Moderate humidity helps a Monstera produce and unfurl leaves. If the air is very dry, a new leaf may become stuck or develop dry edges. First check that watering is consistent and the plant is not beside a heater. If the room remains dry, a humidifier is a more reliable way to raise humidity than spraying the leaves several times a day.

Misting gives only a brief change in humidity. It does not replace watering the roots, and it should not be used to compensate for poor root health. A pebble tray may raise humidity slightly in the small area around the pot, but the effect is limited. Do not allow the pot base to sit in the tray water.

## 6. Feed during active growth

A Monstera uses nutrients as it produces new leaves and roots. During spring and summer, use a balanced liquid houseplant fertiliser according to its label. If the label permits, you can begin with a diluted dose rather than applying a concentrated feed to a plant that has not been fertilised recently.

Feed only when the plant is hydrated and growing. Reduce feeding in autumn and winter if growth slows. Fertiliser cannot make up for weak light, damaged roots or a potting mix that stays wet. Too much fertiliser can cause salt build-up and brown leaf tips or margins. If you suspect excess feed, pause fertilising and review the product instructions before flushing the mix with water.

Do not use garden fertiliser at full strength in a houseplant pot unless it is specifically labelled for that purpose. Follow the manufacturer's application rate and do not combine several products simply because the plant looks slow.

## 7. Clean the leaves and inspect for pests

Large leaves collect dust. Wipe them with a soft damp cloth, supporting the underside as you work. Avoid rough rubbing, household cleaners and leaf-polish products. Cleaning also gives you a chance to inspect the plant closely.

Look under leaves and around the petiole joints for webbing, sticky residue, cotton-like clusters, small bumps or silvery scarring. These can indicate spider mites, mealybugs, scale or thrips. Isolate an affected plant, identify the pest, and use a treatment labelled for indoor ornamental plants. Follow the label exactly and repeat treatment only as directed. Check nearby plants because pests can spread.

## 8. Give a climbing Monstera room to grow

A mature *M. deliciosa* is not a naturally compact tabletop plant. It climbs, produces aerial roots and can develop broad, heavy leaves. A moss pole, coir pole, plank or strong trellis gives the main stem a vertical route. Tie the stem loosely with soft plant ties; do not cinch ties around the leaf stalks.

If the plant leans, check that the support is secure before tightening ties. A heavy plant may need a wider base or a heavier pot. See [how to support a Monstera plant](/articles/support-monstera-plant/) for a complete guide to choosing and installing a support.

## 9. When to prune and repot

Remove dead or fully yellow leaves with clean scissors. If the plant is too large for its space, you can shorten a vine by cutting near a suitable node. Avoid removing too much healthy foliage at once, because leaves supply energy for new growth. Read [how to prune a Monstera plant](/articles/prune-monstera-plant/) before making structural cuts.

Repot when roots fill the pot, grow from the drainage holes, or the mix has broken down and remains wet. Move up only one pot size. A much larger pot can leave a small root ball surrounded by wet soil. If the plant has multiple rooted stems, you may be able to divide it during repotting. Follow [how to repot and split a Monstera](/articles/repot-monstera-plant/).

## 10. A simple Monstera care routine

**Weekly:** Check soil moisture, look for pests, and make sure the pot is not standing in water. Water only when the upper mix has dried enough.

**Monthly during active growth:** Check whether the plant needs feeding, wipe dusty leaves, inspect ties and support, and rotate the pot slightly if growth is leaning toward the window.

**Seasonally:** Reduce watering and feeding when growth slows. Check that the plant is not exposed to cold draughts. In spring, consider whether the roots need more room or whether a long vine needs pruning.

**When something changes:** If several leaves turn yellow, check the roots, soil moisture, drainage and light before assuming the plant needs fertiliser. The University of California's [houseplant problem guide](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/houseplant-problems/) lists several causes of similar symptoms.

## Frequently asked questions about Monstera plant care

### How often should I water my Monstera plant?

There is no reliable weekly schedule for every home. Check the top few centimetres of potting mix and water when that layer has dried. Water thoroughly, then allow the pot to drain completely.

### Should I mist my Monstera every day?

Daily misting is not necessary. It changes humidity only briefly and does not replace root watering. If your room is very dry, use a room humidifier and check that the plant is not near a heater.

### What is the best soil for a Monstera?

Use a loose, airy mix that drains freely while retaining some moisture. Indoor potting mix combined with orchid bark and perlite or pumice is a practical starting point. Avoid dense garden soil and containers without drainage.

### What is the best fertiliser for a Monstera plant?

A balanced liquid houseplant fertiliser is suitable during active growth. Follow its label and avoid feeding a dry, stressed or dormant plant. More fertiliser is not a substitute for suitable light and healthy roots.

### Why are my Monstera leaves turning yellow?

Overwatering and poor drainage are common causes, but low light, cold exposure, pests and nutrient problems can also contribute. Check soil moisture and root condition before changing your routine. See our [yellow-leaf troubleshooting guide](/articles/monstera-yellow-leaves/).

### Does Monstera need direct sunlight?

It usually does best in bright, indirect light. Gentle morning sun may be tolerated after gradual acclimatisation, but strong direct sun can scorch leaves. Variegated types need bright conditions but still need protection from harsh rays.

### How do I make Monstera leaves bigger?

Provide suitable light, healthy roots, enough space and a climbing support. Mature plants often develop larger leaves as they climb, but leaf size also depends on species, genetics and growing conditions. Heavy feeding will not force larger leaves.

### How do I make a Monstera grow faster?

Give it consistent bright indirect light, a free-draining mix, appropriate watering and warmth. Feed modestly during active growth. Growth speed varies with the plant's age, species and season, so avoid trying to accelerate it by overwatering or overfeeding.

### Can I keep a Monstera in a bedroom?

Yes, provided the room has enough indirect daylight and the plant is kept away from cold draughts. Remember that *M. deliciosa* is toxic if chewed by cats or dogs, so consider pet access before choosing the location.

### How do I know when my Monstera needs repotting?

Roots emerging from the drainage holes, a root ball that fills the pot, very rapid drying, or a plant that becomes unstable can indicate it needs a larger container. Repot only one size up and use fresh airy mix.
