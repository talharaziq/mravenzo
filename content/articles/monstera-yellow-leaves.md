**Why is my Monstera plant turning yellow?** Monstera yellow leaves can be caused by too much water, poor drainage, low light, cold conditions, pests or a nutrient problem. Sometimes one old lower leaf simply reaches the end of its life. The important clue is the pattern: one older leaf yellowing slowly is different from several leaves turning yellow in a week.

Do not start by adding fertiliser or watering more. First check the potting mix, drainage and roots. Yellow leaves are a symptom, not a diagnosis, and several different problems can look similar. This guide helps you narrow down the likely cause and choose a sensible next step.

You can also use the [main Monstera plant guide](/articles/monstera-plant-guide/) for light, soil and seasonal growing advice. If the plant is leaning or becoming top-heavy, read [how to support a Monstera plant](/articles/support-monstera-plant/).

## First, work out which leaves are turning yellow

Look at the age and position of the affected leaves. If a single old leaf at the base of the plant is turning yellow while the rest of the plant is healthy and producing new growth, natural ageing may be the explanation. A plant cannot keep every leaf forever.

If several leaves turn yellow together, if new leaves are affected, or if yellowing comes with drooping, soft stems or a persistently wet pot, investigate further. Check whether the yellowing starts on the oldest leaves, appears across the whole plant, or is concentrated on one side. That pattern can help, but it does not prove a single cause.

## 1. Overwatering and poor drainage

Overwatering is one of the first things to check. Monstera roots need oxygen as well as water. When the mix stays saturated, roots can become damaged and may stop taking up water and nutrients effectively. The plant can then develop yellow leaves even though its pot is wet.

**Clues that point to excess moisture:**

- The potting mix remains wet for many days.
- The pot feels heavy even when the plant has not been watered recently.
- Several leaves yellow or droop at the same time.
- The soil smells sour or musty.
- Roots are brown, soft or mushy rather than firm.

**What to do:** Stop watering until the mix has dried to the appropriate level. Check that the pot has drainage holes and that no water is trapped in the outer pot or saucer. If the soil smells bad or the plant is collapsing, slide it out and inspect the roots. Trim clearly dead, mushy roots with clean scissors and repot in a fresh, airy mix in a pot that fits the remaining roots. Do not move the plant into a very large pot.

A healthy watering routine is based on soil moisture, not a fixed calendar. Water thoroughly only when the upper few centimetres have dried. Read [how to care for a Monstera plant](/articles/monstera-plant-care/) for a practical watering method.

## 2. Underwatering or a mix that dries too far

A Monstera that is allowed to become extremely dry can wilt, develop yellowing or brown edges, and lose older leaves. The leaves may feel thinner or curl inward. The pot is often very light and the mix may have pulled away from its edges.

**What to do:** If the soil is dry, water slowly and evenly until the whole root ball is moistened and excess water drains out. Very dry peat-based mix can sometimes repel water, so apply water in stages rather than pouring it all at once. If water runs down the gap around the root ball without wetting it, rehydrate the mix gradually. After that, return to checking soil moisture before watering again.

Do not respond to a dry plant by leaving it sitting in a bowl of water for days. The goal is to rehydrate the root ball, drain the pot and then let the upper layer dry before the next watering.

## 3. Not enough light

Low light can cause slow growth, long gaps between leaves and pale or yellow-green foliage. A Monstera in a dark corner uses water more slowly, so the soil may also stay wet longer. That means a plant can show signs of low light and overwatering at the same time.

Move the plant to a brighter position with filtered daylight. Avoid shifting it directly from a dim room into intense sun. Make the change gradually and watch for signs of leaf scorch. If a window is very bright, a little distance from the glass or a sheer curtain can help.

The University of California's [houseplant problem guide](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/houseplant-problems/) notes that yellowing and weak growth can have several causes, including insufficient light and poor root health. That is why it is worth checking the full growing conditions rather than treating the leaf colour alone.

## 4. Cold draughts or sudden temperature changes

Monsteras are tropical plants. A cold windowsill, an exterior door that opens frequently, or a stream of air from an air conditioner can stress the leaves and roots. Cold conditions also slow water use, which makes an already wet mix more problematic.

Move the plant away from cold glass and draughts. Keep it in a stable, warm room and avoid placing it directly beside a heater. Do not try to correct cold stress by adding fertiliser; wait until the plant is growing again before resuming normal feeding.

## 5. Nutrient imbalance or too much fertiliser

A lack of nutrients can contribute to pale or yellow leaves, especially when the plant has been in the same depleted potting mix for a long time. However, yellowing is not proof that the plant needs fertiliser. Damaged roots cannot take up nutrients properly, and excess fertiliser can also cause problems.

Look for a wider pattern of weak growth rather than one isolated old leaf. Check when the plant was last fed and whether the product was used at the label rate. Too much fertiliser can leave soluble salts in the mix and contribute to brown tips or margins.

If the plant has healthy roots and is actively growing, feed with a balanced houseplant fertiliser at the manufacturer's recommended dilution. Do not feed a plant with soggy soil, root rot or severe stress. Correct those problems first.

## 6. Pests on the leaves or stems

Spider mites, thrips, scale and mealybugs can damage Monstera foliage. Spider mites may cause fine pale speckling and webbing. Thrips can leave silvery or scarred patches. Scale insects may look like small fixed bumps, while mealybugs often resemble white cottony clusters around joints.

Inspect both sides of the leaves, the petiole bases and the growing points. Isolate the plant from other houseplants if you find pests. Identify the pest before choosing a treatment, then follow the product label for indoor ornamental plants. Repeat applications only as directed, because many treatments do not affect every life stage of an insect.

## 7. Pot-bound roots or old potting mix

A Monstera with a dense root ball may dry very quickly, become unstable or stop growing well. Old potting mix can also compact and lose the air spaces roots need. These issues may contribute to leaf discolouration, although they are not the only possible causes.

Check the drainage holes and look for roots circling tightly around the root ball. If the plant is pot-bound, move it into a container only one size larger and refresh the mix. Avoid jumping to a very large pot. Follow our guide to [repotting and splitting a Monstera](/articles/repot-monstera-plant/).

## A step-by-step troubleshooting check

1. **Check soil moisture.** Is the root zone wet, evenly moist or very dry?
2. **Check drainage.** Does the pot have open holes, and is the saucer empty?
3. **Check the roots if symptoms are severe.** Healthy roots are generally firm; mushy, dark roots may be rotting.
4. **Review light and temperature.** Has the plant recently been moved, shaded or exposed to a draught?
5. **Inspect for pests.** Look closely at leaf undersides and stem joints.
6. **Review fertiliser use.** Has the plant been fed too often or not at all during active growth?
7. **Change one main factor at a time.** This makes it easier to see whether the plant is improving.

Do not expect a yellow leaf to turn green again. Once the tissue has lost its colour, it usually will not recover. Remove a leaf when it is mostly yellow or dead, using clean scissors near the petiole base. Keep some healthy foliage so the plant can continue to photosynthesise.

## When should you repot a yellowing Monstera?

Repot if you find root rot, a collapsed potting mix, or a root ball that has filled the container. Do not repot automatically after every yellow leaf. Repotting adds stress, and a plant with one naturally ageing leaf may not need any intervention.

If root rot is present, remove the plant from the pot, discard the saturated mix, trim only mushy roots and use a clean pot with drainage. If roots are firm and the mix drains well, adjust the watering or light routine first. A detailed guide is available at [how to repot a Monstera plant](/articles/repot-monstera-plant/).

## Frequently asked questions about yellow Monstera leaves

### Should I cut yellow leaves off my Monstera?

You can remove a leaf once it is mostly yellow or dead. Use clean scissors and cut the leaf stalk near its base without damaging the main stem. First check why it turned yellow.

### Can yellow Monstera leaves turn green again?

A leaf that has fully yellowed usually will not turn green again. Focus on protecting healthy leaves and correcting the cause of the yellowing.

### Does overwatering always make Monstera leaves yellow?

No. Overwatering can cause yellowing, but so can underwatering, low light, cold stress, pests and nutrient problems. Check soil moisture and root condition before deciding.

### Why are only the bottom leaves turning yellow?

One older lower leaf may be ageing naturally. If several lower leaves yellow quickly, check watering, drainage and root health.

### Why are new Monstera leaves turning yellow?

Yellowing new growth deserves a closer check. Possible causes include overwatering, root damage, a nutrient-uptake issue, pests or sudden environmental stress. Inspect the roots and growing point rather than adding fertiliser immediately.

### Can too much sunlight turn Monstera leaves yellow?

Intense direct sun can cause pale, bleached or scorched patches that may later turn brown. Move the plant to bright filtered light and acclimatise it gradually if the position changes.

### Should I fertilise a Monstera with yellow leaves?

Only if you have reason to believe the plant needs nutrients and its roots are healthy. Fertiliser will not fix root rot, soggy soil or low light, and excess feed can damage roots.

### How long does a Monstera take to recover?

It depends on the cause and how much root damage has occurred. You may see stable growth before the old leaves look better, because damaged yellow tissue does not usually recover. Watch for healthy new growth rather than expecting every old leaf to change colour.

### Why is my Monstera yellow and drooping at the same time?

Both overwatering and severe underwatering can cause drooping and yellowing. Feel the soil and inspect the roots if the pot has remained wet. Do not water again until you know whether the root ball is dry or saturated.
