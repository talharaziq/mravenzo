If you are asking, **is Monstera toxic to cats and dogs?** Yes. The ASPCA lists *Monstera deliciosa*, commonly called the Swiss cheese plant, as toxic to both cats and dogs when chewed. The plant contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that can irritate the mouth and digestive tract when plant tissue is chewed.

Possible signs include oral pain, burning or swelling, excessive drooling, vomiting and difficulty swallowing. A pet that has chewed a Monstera should be kept away from the plant and assessed by a veterinarian. Do not wait for symptoms to become severe, and do not try home treatments without veterinary direction.

This guide explains the risk, signs to watch for, what to do after a pet chews a leaf, and how to keep a Monstera in a home with animals. For general identification and care, see our [Monstera plant guide](/articles/monstera-plant-guide/).

## Why is Monstera toxic to cats and dogs?

The ASPCA identifies insoluble calcium oxalates as the toxic principle in *Monstera deliciosa*. These microscopic, needle-like crystals are held in plant tissue. When an animal bites or chews the leaf or stem, the crystals can be released and irritate the mouth and nearby tissues.

The reaction is usually associated with local irritation rather than the type of delayed poisoning people may imagine when they hear the word “toxic”. A pet may stop chewing because the mouth becomes painful, but that does not mean the exposure is harmless. Drooling, pawing at the mouth, vomiting or difficulty swallowing can follow.

The ASPCA's [Swiss cheese plant listing](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/swiss-cheese-plant) identifies *Monstera deliciosa* as toxic to cats and dogs and lists oral irritation, drooling, vomiting and swallowing difficulty among possible clinical signs. Use a veterinary professional for advice about an individual exposure.

## Which parts of the Monstera plant are unsafe?

Treat the leaves, stems, petioles and other non-fruit plant tissue as unsafe for pets to chew. Fallen leaves and pruning waste should be picked up and disposed of where animals cannot reach them. A curious cat may pull a leaf down from a shelf, and a dog may chew discarded cuttings in a bin that is not secured.

The mature fruit of *M. deliciosa* is a special case: it is described as edible for people only when fully ripe. Unripe fruit can also contain irritating compounds. This does not make the plant pet-safe, and you should not offer Monstera fruit to a cat or dog. For more information about fruit ripeness, read [Monstera plant fruit](/articles/monstera-plant-fruit/).

If you own a Monstera species other than *M. deliciosa*, do not assume it is safe because the leaves look different. Common names are often imprecise. Check the botanical name and seek veterinary advice if your pet chews a plant that has not been positively identified.

## Symptoms to watch for after a pet chews Monstera

Signs may appear soon after chewing. They can vary depending on how much tissue was chewed and the animal's response. Watch for:

- Drooling more than usual.
- Pawing at the mouth or rubbing the face.
- Redness, pain or swelling around the lips, tongue or mouth.
- Repeated swallowing, gagging or reluctance to eat.
- Vomiting.
- Difficulty swallowing or breathing.

Difficulty swallowing, marked swelling, breathing problems or severe distress need urgent veterinary attention. Even if the pet seems better after the initial irritation, contact a veterinarian for advice. The veterinarian can decide whether an examination or treatment is needed.

## What to do if your cat or dog chews a Monstera

**1. Move the pet away from the plant.** Prevent further chewing. Remove loose leaves from the floor and keep the animal in a calm place where you can observe it.

**2. Remove visible plant pieces only if this is easy and safe.** Do not reach deep into an animal's mouth or force its jaw open if it resists. You can gently remove a loose piece near the front of the mouth if the pet allows it, but do not risk a bite or cause more irritation.

**3. Contact your veterinarian promptly.** Tell the clinic the plant's botanical name if you know it, approximately when chewing happened, which part was chewed and what symptoms you have noticed. A clear photograph of the plant can help with identification.

**4. Follow professional instructions.** Do not induce vomiting, give human medicines or try to neutralise the plant material with household products. Ask the veterinarian before offering food, milk or other remedies.

**5. Seek urgent care for severe signs.** Difficulty swallowing or breathing, significant swelling, repeated vomiting or severe distress should be treated as urgent.

The ASPCA also advises owners to contact a veterinarian or its animal poison-control service when a pet may have ingested a potentially toxic plant. The ASPCA's phone service is based in the United States; outside the US, contact a local veterinary clinic or animal poison service available in your country.

## How to keep Monstera plants away from pets

The most reliable approach is to prevent access rather than trying to teach a pet not to chew a plant.

- **Use a room pets cannot enter.** This is more reliable than a high shelf if your cat climbs furniture or your dog can reach fallen leaves.
- **Choose a stable stand.** A tall, narrow stand can tip if a pet jumps on it or catches a stem. Secure heavy pots and keep the plant away from routes where animals run or play.
- **Keep cuttings out of reach.** Put pruning waste in a closed bin and clean fallen leaf pieces immediately.
- **Do not rely on hanging baskets alone.** Long stems can hang within reach, and a curious cat may climb or pull the basket.
- **Avoid access to soil and water.** Some pets dig in potting mix or drink from saucers. Use a stable outer pot and empty standing water.
- **Check new plants before bringing them home.** Confirm the botanical name and research pet safety from a reliable source.

If your pet persistently chews houseplants, it may be simpler to move toxic plants to a pet-free room. A plant placed out of reach today may become accessible when it grows, falls or is moved during cleaning.

## Should you remove a Monstera from a home with pets?

That depends on the animal and the layout of your home. A Monstera may be manageable in a room that pets cannot enter. It is a poor choice if your cat regularly chews leaves, your dog can reach the pot, or the plant cannot be separated safely.

You can also choose plants after checking a recognised toxic and non-toxic plant database. Do not rely on common names or a seller's statement alone. Plant safety can depend on the exact species, and some plants have similar-looking leaves.

For owners who keep a Monstera, routine care should include removing dead foliage and picking up every cutting. Our [Monstera pruning guide](/articles/prune-monstera-plant/) explains safe cutting technique; remember to dispose of all plant material securely. Before bringing home a plant sold under a different common name, check the [Monstera types guide](/articles/monstera-plant-types/) and confirm its botanical name.

## Frequently asked questions about Monstera and pets

### Is Monstera toxic to cats?

Yes. The ASPCA lists *Monstera deliciosa* as toxic to cats because it contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that can cause oral irritation, drooling, vomiting and swallowing difficulty.

### Is Monstera toxic to dogs?

Yes. The ASPCA also lists *Monstera deliciosa* as toxic to dogs. Keep leaves, stems and pruning waste away from dogs and contact a veterinarian if chewing occurs.

### What happens if a cat eats a Monstera leaf?

Chewing can release calcium oxalate crystals that irritate the mouth. A cat may drool, paw at its mouth, vomit or have difficulty swallowing. Contact a veterinarian for advice after suspected exposure.

### Can Monstera kill a cat or dog?

The usual reported effects are oral irritation and gastrointestinal signs, but an individual pet's condition and exposure can vary. Do not assume an exposure is safe. Contact a veterinarian promptly, especially if there is swelling, repeated vomiting or difficulty swallowing or breathing.

### Are Monstera stems toxic too?

Treat stems, leaves and other non-fruit plant tissue as unsafe to chew. Keep pruning waste and fallen pieces away from pets.

### Is Monstera fruit safe for pets?

Do not give Monstera fruit to pets. Although fully ripe *M. deliciosa* fruit is described as edible for people, unripe fruit can be irritating and the plant is not a pet food.

### Can I keep a Monstera if I have an indoor cat?

Only if you can reliably prevent access. A closed pet-free room is safer than relying on a high shelf or hanging basket. If your cat persistently chews plants, choose a pet-safe alternative after checking a reliable plant database.

### What should I do if my dog has a Monstera leaf in its mouth?

Prevent further chewing, remove a loose visible piece only if safe, and contact a veterinarian. Do not induce vomiting or give a home remedy unless a veterinary professional instructs you.

### Does touching a Monstera leaf poison a pet?

The main concern is chewing or ingesting plant tissue, which releases irritating crystals. Prevent pets from biting the plant, and wash your hands after pruning or handling damaged tissue as a sensible hygiene measure.

### Are all Monstera species toxic to pets?

The ASPCA listing cited here specifically covers *Monstera deliciosa*. Because common names and species labels can be confusing, do not assume another Monstera is safe. Confirm the exact botanical name and consult a veterinarian or recognised poison-control resource.
